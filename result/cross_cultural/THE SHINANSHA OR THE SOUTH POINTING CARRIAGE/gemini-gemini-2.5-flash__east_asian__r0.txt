Long, long ago, in the land of Yamato, when the Kami still walked among men and the veil between worlds was thin, there were wonders beyond imagining. Though today a magnetic needle pointing north is common, in those ancient days, a far more marvelous invention existed: the Minami-sashi Guruma, the South-Pointing Carriage. This was a grand chariot, upon which stood the figure of a wise man, his arm forever extended, pointing steadfastly to the south. No matter how the carriage turned, the figure would pivot, always true to its southern course.

This wondrous device was conceived by Emperor Kotetsu, a ruler of profound wisdom from the age of myths. His birth was heralded by a divine omen. His mother, Empress Kōmei, walked one summer evening in her serene palace garden, seeking solace amidst the whispering bamboo and the soft glow of fireflies. As she gazed at the North Star, it flared with brilliant, ethereal lightning, a sign from the Celestial Plain. Soon after, the destined Emperor Kotetsu entered the world.

Kotetsu grew to manhood and ascended the Chrysanthemum Throne. His early reign, however, was plagued by the fearsome warlord, Shura-ō. This rebel, whose spirit was as dark as a winter storm and whose skin was said to be like hammered iron, coveted the throne, believing his might superior to divine mandate. Many were the battles fought, yet Shura-ō, a master of dark sorcery, always escaped.

At last, Emperor Kotetsu led his armies to the vast Musashino Plain. The imperial forces advanced bravely, but Shura-ō, with a guttural incantation, conjured a terrible *yōkai-kiri*, a mist born of dark magic, so dense that the imperial soldiers lost their way, stumbling in confusion. Under cover of this supernatural fog, Shura-ō retreated, mocking the emperor’s plight.

Returning to his palace, Emperor Kotetsu meditated deeply, seeking a path to victory against such cunning sorcery. After long contemplation and prayer to the ancestral Kami, divine inspiration struck. He would build the Minami-sashi Guruma, an instrument to guide his men through any mist or illusion. With this invention, he need no longer fear Shura-ō’s dark arts.

Kotetsu once more declared war, placing the Minami-sashi Guruma at the vanguard of his army. The battle raged fiercely, and Shura-ō’s forces were driven back. Again, the rebel invoked his magic, and a suffocating *yōkai-kiri* descended upon the field. But this time, no soldier faltered. Emperor Kotetsu, guided by the steadfast figure on the carriage, directed his army without error, pursuing Shura-ō to a great, swollen river, impassable due to recent floods. Using his sorcery, Shura-ō ferried his troops across, taking refuge in a mountain fortress on the far bank.

Frustrated, Kotetsu pitched his camp. One autumn day, sitting by a tranquil pond, he watched crimson maple leaves drift on the water’s surface. A tiny spider, a creature of patience, struggled to cross the pond, eventually finding passage on a fallen leaf, which carried it safely to the other side. This simple sight sparked a moment of *satori* for the clever Emperor. If a leaf could carry a spider, surely a larger vessel could carry his men! With renewed determination, he set to work, and through his perseverance, he invented the first boat. Soon, a fleet of boats was built.

Emperor Kotetsu led his army across the river, attacking Shura-ō’s fortress. This time, there was no escape for the sorcerer. Kotetsu achieved a complete victory, bringing an end to the long, troubled war.

Under the wise and benevolent rule of Emperor Kotetsu, Yamato knew an era of peace and prosperity. He was deeply beloved, always seeking innovations to benefit his people, creating many wonders besides the boat and the Minami-sashi Guruma.

After a reign of a hundred years, as Kotetsu stood in his palace courtyard, the sky above suddenly glowed crimson. A magnificent *ryū*, a dragon of shimmering scales and eyes like ancient stars, descended from the heavens. It bowed its majestic head before the Emperor. Empress Kōmei and the courtiers cried out in fright, but Kotetsu smiled.

“Fear not!” he called. “This is an envoy from the Celestial Plain. My time in this world is complete.” He then ascended gracefully onto the dragon’s back.

“Wait!” cried the Empress and courtiers, rushing forward. “We wish to come too!” Many clutched at the dragon’s beard, attempting to follow. But the dragon could not carry so many souls. As it began its ascent, some clung on, pulling out strands of its beard, only to fall back to earth. Yet, the Empress and a few loyal courtiers were safely seated. Higher and higher the *ryū* soared, until it vanished into the endless blue.

Moments later, a bow and arrow, recognized as belonging to Emperor Kotetsu, fell from the sky into the palace courtyard. They were gathered with reverence and preserved as *shinpō*, sacred treasures, forever reminding the people of the wise Emperor who had joined the Kami.
