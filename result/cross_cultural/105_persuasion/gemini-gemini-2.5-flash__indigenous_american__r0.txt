Listen, my children, and hear a tale of the River's Bend Territory, of a time when the Old Ways still held sway, though some hearts clung to shadows.

There was an elder, Walter of the Cedar Lodge Clan, a man whose spirit found no greater joy than in tracing his lineage back through the generations. He held no sacred bundle, no painted hide of ancestors to gaze upon; his treasure was in the telling of his own name, "Walter of the Cedar Lodge," and the long line of respected ones from whom he sprang. When the winds of worry blew through his lodge, he would speak the names of his forebears, and his spirit would swell with pride, forgetting the small troubles of the day. He would recall how the Cedar Lodge Clan had first settled by the Great River, how their warriors had served with honor, how their daughters had brought strong bloodlines. And above all, he would speak of himself, Walter, a man blessed by the Great Spirit with a strong frame and pleasing features, a gift he valued only second to the ancient line that flowed through his veins.

His first wife, Gentle Heart Woman, had been a spirit of kindness and good sense, a woman whose wisdom had softened his sharp edges and quietly upheld the family's standing for many seasons. Though her own heart knew quiet sorrows, she found strength in her duties, her friends, and her three daughters: Elder Daughter Willow, Quiet Waters, and Little Lark. When her spirit journeyed to the Ancestor World, she left her girls, still young, in the care of a father whose vanity often clouded his judgment. Yet, she had a trusted friend, Wise Woman Raven, who lived close by the Cedar Lodge, and upon her counsel, Gentle Heart Woman had placed her hope for her daughters' guidance.

***

In time, Little Lark found herself at the Saltwater Gathering, recovering from a stumble. Her spirit was mended, though her body remained tender, and the journey home to the River's Bend was still a challenge. Her mother and father, the Musgrove Clan of the High Meadow, were eager to return for the winter feasts, but it seemed Little Lark would need to stay longer. Yet, her time at the Saltwater Gathering was not one of hardship. The people of the shore, the Harville family, had shown great generosity, sharing their hearth and their food. Little Lark had found much to enjoy: the vastness of the Saltwater, the sun on her face, the many faces at the sacred ceremonies, and the stories shared by the wise ones of the village.

When Quiet Waters asked after Brave Spirit Benwick, Little Lark's face clouded. Young Deer, her husband, laughed. "Oh, Brave Spirit is well, I think, but he is a strange one! We asked him to return with us to the High Meadow, and he seemed pleased, but then, with many excuses, he refused. He said he did not hunt, that he had been misunderstood. But in truth," Young Deer said, turning to Quiet Waters, "he thought you, Quiet Waters, would be close by. When he learned Wise Woman Raven's lodge was a three-day journey away, his spirit failed him. He lacked the courage to come." Little Lark grumbled, perhaps not believing Brave Spirit worthy of an Elliot, or perhaps not wishing Quiet Waters to be more of a draw than herself. But Quiet Waters, with her gentle heart, felt only warmth that her spirit was admired. "He speaks of your wisdom," Young Deer added, "and the many tales you shared with him."

***

Elder Walter, though his schemes had twisted and turned, still sought his own comfort. He soon left the Healing Springs. And when Sly Fox Woman was later found living under his protection in the Great Trading Post in the Far Lands, it became clear how many paths he had walked, seeking to secure his own standing. Sly Fox Woman, it seemed, had allowed her heart to guide her, sacrificing her own cleverness for the young chief. Yet, her spirit was strong, and it was whispered that her cunning might yet outwit his, and that she might become the wife of Young Walter after all.

Elder Walter and Elder Daughter Willow were left with a hollow feeling, their companion gone, their trust betrayed. They sought comfort in their distant cousins, but to flatter others without being praised in return is a cold meal indeed.

Quiet Waters, whose spirit had long known where her true happiness lay with Strong Current Wentworth, felt only one shadow upon her joy: that her own lineage offered little of renown to match his. The difference in their possessions meant nothing; it was the lack of a respected clan to welcome him, to offer in return the warmth and honor that his own brothers and sisters so freely gave her. She had only two friends to offer to his circle: Wise Woman Raven and Healer Woman Swift Hand. To these, Strong Current Wentworth was glad to bind his heart. Wise Woman Raven, despite her past mistakes, he now held in high regard. And Healer Woman Swift Hand, for her recent kindness to Quiet Waters, had earned his lasting respect. Their marriage, far from taking a friend from Healer Woman, had given her two. Strong Current Wentworth, with the fierce heart of a warrior and the loyalty of a true friend, helped her reclaim her ancestral lands in the Warm Southern Isles, writing for her, speaking for her, clearing her path through all the small troubles.

Healer Woman Swift Hand's joys were not dimmed by her new fortune and health, nor by the companionship of such friends. Her cheerfulness never faltered, and with these gifts of the spirit, she could have faced any worldly challenge. Quiet Waters, too, found her spring of happiness in the warmth of her heart, and Strong Current Wentworth cherished that tenderness. His life as a Sea-Hunter was the only thing that gave her friends pause, the dread of a coming storm, a distant war, the only cloud on her bright sky. She gloried in being a Sea-Hunter's wife, though she paid the toll of quick alarm, belonging to a path that asks as much courage in the lodge as on the open waters.

And so, the seasons turned.
