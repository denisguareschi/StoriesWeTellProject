Once upon a time, in a verdant valley where a winding brook met a sun-dappled meadow, there lived a wily Ape and a simple-hearted Crab. One bright autumn day, as they played by the water's edge, the Crab unearthed a plump, sweet bun, while the Ape, with a boastful grin, held aloft a curious, hard pip.

“See what a treasure I have found!” exclaimed the Crab, holding up his delicious discovery.

“And I, too, have something of great worth!” countered the Ape, displaying his pip.

Now, the Ape, though he loved sweet fruits, knew this pip was as inedible as a stone. His greedy heart, however, yearned for the Crab’s tasty bun. “My dear fellow,” he began, his voice dripping with false concern, “how short-sighted you are! Your bun will be gone in a moment, a fleeting pleasure. But this tiny pip, if planted, will grow into a magnificent tree, laden with golden fruit year after year! Imagine the bounty! I vow, if you do not believe me, I shall plant it myself, though I fear you will live to regret such folly.”

The honest Crab, swayed by the Ape’s clever words, could not resist such a grand promise of future abundance. He agreed to the exchange. The cunning Ape swiftly devoured the bun, then reluctantly passed the pip to the Crab, lest he provoke the creature’s sharp pincers. They parted ways, the Ape retreating to his woodland canopy, the Crab scuttling home to his burrow by the brook. There, with diligent care, he planted the pip.

Spring arrived, and to the Crab’s delight, a tiny shoot emerged. Each year it grew taller, until one glorious autumn, it bore fruit – great, round orbs that ripened from green to a deep, burnished amber. The little Crab would sit beneath its branches, his keen eyes observing the luscious jewels, dreaming of their sweet taste.

At last, convinced the fruit was perfectly ripe, the Crab tried to climb the tree, but his legs, made for scuttling, were useless for ascending the smooth trunk. In his dilemma, he remembered his old playmate, the Ape, who was the finest climber in all the woods. He resolved to seek his help.

Scuttling across the stones and through the shadowed glades, the Crab found the Ape dozing in an ancient oak. The Ape, roused by the Crab’s tale, feigned delight. “Ah, my old friend! A tree, you say? And laden with fruit? I shall assist you at once!” But in his heart, he harboured a wicked plan to claim all the bounty for himself.

He clambered up the tree with ease, but instead of picking ripe fruit for the waiting Crab, he plucked the hardest, greenest ones and hurled them down, striking the defenceless creature again and again until, alas, the poor Crab lay still, crushed beneath a hail of unripe fruit. The wicked Ape, seized by a coward’s fear, fled into the depths of the forest.

Not long after, the Crab’s young son, Cecil, discovered his father’s lifeless form, battered and broken. His grief soon turned to burning rage and a solemn vow of vengeance. He knew the Ape was the culprit, remembering his father’s story of the pip and the bun, and the Ape’s insatiable greed. But the Ape was strong and cunning; Cecil knew he could not face him alone.

He sought out his father’s old friends: the sturdy Millstone, the diligent Bee, and the fiery Chestnut. Each, upon hearing the tragic tale, readily pledged their aid. Together, they devised a cunning trap to bring the villain to justice.

A few days later, a messenger, a small beetle, arrived at the Ape’s dwelling. “My young master, the son of Barnaby Crab, requests the honour of your presence at a solemn wake for his late father. He begs you, as one of his father’s dearest friends, to attend.”

The Ape’s heart swelled with smug satisfaction. “A wake, you say?” he feigned surprise, even squeezing out a few false tears. “Alas, I am grieved to hear of my old friend’s passing! I shall be delighted to pay my respects.” He looked forward to a grand feast, unaware of the snare laid for him.

He arrived at the Crab’s humble cottage, where young Cecil greeted him with polite, if cold, ceremony. After a luxurious feast, the Ape was invited to a quiet parlour for a cordial. He waited, growing impatient, and approached the warm hearth to pour himself a drink. Suddenly, a fiery ember, the very Chestnut himself, sprang from the ashes and struck the rogue a searing blow to the neck!

Screaming, the Ape stumbled back and made for the door. But as he burst through, the diligent Bee darted forth, planting a venomous sting upon his cheek. Chattering with pain and fury, the Ape bolted towards the gate, only for the heavy Millstone, perched precariously above, to tumble down with a mighty crash, crushing the villain’s head.

As the Ape lay helpless, young Cecil approached, his great pincers poised. “Do you now remember, wretch, how you murdered my father?”

“It was… your father’s… fault!” gasped the unrepentant Ape, his voice broken.

“Still you lie!” cried Cecil, and with a swift, terrible snap of his mighty pincers, he exacted the final justice, severing the villain’s head. Thus, the wicked Ape met his deserved end, and young Cecil avenged his father’s death, bringing peace to the valley once more.
