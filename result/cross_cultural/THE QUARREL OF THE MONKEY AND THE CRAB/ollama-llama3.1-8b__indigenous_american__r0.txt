Listen well, dear ones, as I share with you the tale of the cunning monkey and the brave crab. It is a story of deceit, greed, and ultimately, justice.

In the ancient days, when the forests were young and the rivers flowed strong, a mischievous monkey and a clever crab lived along the winding riverbank. One autumn day, as the leaves rustled in the gentle breeze, the monkey and the crab played together, their laughter echoing through the trees.

The monkey, with a twinkle in his eye, boasted of finding a persimmon-seed, hard and unyielding as a stone. The crab, however, held a plump rice-dumpling, its steam rising like a mist from the earth. The monkey, driven by his insatiable hunger, proposed an exchange, offering to trade the seed for the dumpling. The crab, though wary, was taken in by the monkey's cunning words, and the exchange was made.

As the seasons passed, the seed took root, and a young tree grew, its branches stretching towards the sky. The crab watched with wonder as the tree bloomed and bore fruit, its persimmons ripening to a deep orange. But the monkey, now aware of the tree's bounty, devised a plan to claim the fruit for himself.

With wicked intent, the monkey pulled off the ripe persimmons, one by one, and hurled them at the defenseless crab, covering him in wounds. The crab lay still, his shell broken, his body battered. The monkey, satisfied with his cruel deed, fled the scene, leaving the young crab to mourn his father's passing.

The young crab, with a heart full of sorrow and a spirit burning with justice, vowed to avenge his father's death. He called upon his friends, the mortar, the bee, and the chestnut, to aid him in his quest for revenge.

Meanwhile, the monkey, still basking in his cruel deed, received an invitation to the young crab's father's memorial feast. He attended the ceremony, feigning sorrow and gratitude, but secretly rejoicing in his own wickedness.

But the young crab and his friends were not fooled. They had set a trap, and as the monkey reached for the tea kettle, the chestnut burst forth from the ashes, striking the monkey with a searing pain. The bee stung him on the cheek, and the mortar, hidden above the gate, crashed down upon his head.

As the monkey lay crushed and helpless, the young crab approached, his claws raised high. "Do you now remember that you murdered my father?" he asked, his voice firm and steady.

The monkey, broken and defiant, met his well-deserved punishment. His head was severed, and his wicked spirit was finally at peace.

Thus, the tale of the monkey and the crab comes to an end, a reminder of the consequences of greed and deceit. May it serve as a cautionary story, guiding us to live in harmony with nature and to respect the delicate balance of the world around us.
