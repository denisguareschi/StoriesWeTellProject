In the time before memory, when creatures spoke with the Great Spirit, Little Brother Monkey, sly, met Elder Crab by the Singing River one crisp autumn day.

Elder Crab found a sweet berry cake. "Look!" he called. Monkey held up a hard fruit seed. Greedy, he yearned for the cake. "Let us trade," he proposed. "Your cake is for today, but my seed promises many fruits." Crab, trusting, traded his prize. Monkey devoured the cake, giving the seed reluctantly. They parted, Monkey to the Great Forest, Crab to plant the seed.

With next spring, a shoot reached for the Sun Father. It grew, till one autumn, it bore fine fruits. Elder Crab watched them ripen, but his legs could not climb. He remembered Trickster Monkey, the climber, and sought him out.

He found Monkey resting. Monkey's eyes gleamed with wicked thought. He climbed, but instead of sharing, tore hard fruits from branches, raining them upon Elder Crab until life left him. Elder Crab lay broken, spirit flown. Trickster Monkey fled.

Not far, Young Crab, son of the Elder, found his father broken, spirit gone. He wept, then resolved justice, knowing Trickster Monkey was the murderer. Alone, he sought allies: Stone Grandfather, Buzzing One the Bee, and Hard-Shell Brother the Chestnut. All pledged aid. They planned balance, then laid Elder Crab to rest.

Meanwhile, Trickster Monkey chuckled, believing his cleverness had won. Yet a cold fear gnawed. "Who saw?" he thought. "The wind tells no tales." He stayed hidden for days, but the forest called. "No one knows," he convinced himself.

When the moon had turned seven times, a messenger arrived. "Young Crab invites you to honor his father's spirit, remembering you as a dear friend." Monkey's fears vanished. He feigned sorrow. "I will surely come!" He shed false tears, but the messenger knew true pain awaited him.

Monkey smoothed his fur and set out. Greeted by the Crabs, he ate a generous meal. Afterwards, Young Crab invited him to a quiet lodge. Monkey impatiently approached the hearth fire. From the embers, Hard-Shell Brother burst forth, striking Monkey's neck. Monkey howled and fled. Buzzing One, hidden, stung his cheek. Screaming, Monkey ran towards the gate. There, atop, Stone Grandfather waited, plummeting down, striking Monkey's head. He lay broken, spirit trapped in pain.

Young Crab approached, holding sharp claws over the fallen Trickster. "Remember my father, whose life you took?" "You... are the avenger?" gasped broken Monkey. "Of course," said Young Crab. "It was his own foolishness! Not mine!" cried the Trickster. "Still you lie?" With a swift, clean strike, Young Crab ended the Trickster's breath.

And so, balance was restored, justice found its way, a teaching for all. That is the end of this telling.
