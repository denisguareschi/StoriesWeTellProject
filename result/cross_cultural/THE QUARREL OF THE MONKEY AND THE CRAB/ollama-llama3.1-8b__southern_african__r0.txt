Hear the tale of the quarrel between the mischievous Mabolelo, a cunning monkey, and the gentle Makhulu, a wise crab. It is a story of friendship, deceit, and vengeance, set against the backdrop of the savannah's golden grasslands and the river's winding waters.

Long ago, in the time of the great rains, Mabolelo and Makhulu were inseparable companions. They would spend their days exploring the riverbanks, chasing after fish, and basking in the warm sunlight. One bright autumn morning, as they were strolling along the river's edge, Mabolelo spotted a succulent rice-dumpling and Makhulu's eyes landed on a persimmon seed. The two friends, eager to share in each other's good fortune, proposed a trade. Mabolelo, convinced of his own cleverness, convinced Makhulu to exchange his precious rice-dumpling for the persimmon seed.

The seed, as Mabolelo had promised, soon sprouted into a magnificent tree, its branches heavy with juicy persimmons. But Mabolelo's greed got the better of him, and he began to plot against his friend. He convinced himself that the persimmons were ripe and ready to eat, and with a wicked glint in his eye, he began to pluck them from the tree. Each persimmon he picked, he hurled at Makhulu, who lay helpless beneath the tree's branches. The poor crab's body was soon battered and bruised, his shell cracked and broken.

When Mabolelo had finished his cruel work, he fled the scene, leaving Makhulu's lifeless body to rot beneath the tree. But the young Makhulu, a fierce and determined soul, vowed to avenge his father's death. He gathered his loyal friends – the wise mortar, the cunning bee, and the stalwart chestnut – and together they hatched a plan to bring Mabolelo to justice.

The monkey, unaware of the danger lurking in the shadows, accepted the young crab's invitation to attend the funeral feast. He arrived at the ceremony, his face painted with false tears, and his heart full of deceit. But the young crab's friends had other plans. As Mabolelo sat at the feast, sipping his tea and pretending to mourn Makhulu's passing, the chestnut burst forth from the fireplace, striking him with a scorching hot coal. The bee followed, stinging him with a painful blow, and the mortar, hidden above the gate, came crashing down, crushing the monkey's skull.

As Mabolelo lay dying, the young crab approached him, his pitcher claws at the ready. "Do you now remember that you murdered my father?" he asked, his voice firm and unwavering. Mabolelo's response was a feeble gasp, a final attempt to shift the blame onto the deceased Makhulu. But it was too late. The young crab's wrath was unrelenting, and with one swift stroke, he cut off Mabolelo's head, bringing an end to the wicked monkey's reign of terror.

Thus, the story of Mabolelo and Makhulu comes to an end, a cautionary tale of the dangers of greed and deceit, and the importance of justice and revenge in a world governed by the laws of nature. May the spirits of the land guide us on our own paths, and may we always remember the lessons of this ancient tale.
