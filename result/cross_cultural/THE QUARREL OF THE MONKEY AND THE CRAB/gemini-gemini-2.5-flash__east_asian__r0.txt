Mukashi mukashi, haruka naru inishie no koro, in a land where the rivers flowed with clear water and the mountains were cloaked in ancient pines, there lived a cunning Saru-san, a monkey with a face as red as a ripe persimmon, and a humble Kani-san, a diligent crab who dwelled by the riverbank.

One bright autumn day, as they played by the water's edge, Kani-san unearthed a plump *onigiri*, a rice-dumpling, while Saru-san found only a hard *kaki no tane*, a persimmon seed. Though the monkey loved *kaki* above all fruits, this seed was useless to him. His heart, however, swelled with envy at the sight of the crab’s delicious *onigiri*.

"Ah, Kani-san," chattered Saru-san, his eyes glinting with mischief. "How short-sighted you are! This *onigiri* is but a fleeting pleasure. But my seed, if planted, will grow into a mighty *kaki* tree, bearing golden fruits year after year. Think of the bounty! Exchange it with me, and you shall have endless sweetness."

Kani-san, simple of heart and easily swayed by such clever words, agreed to the exchange. Saru-san devoured the *onigiri* in a single gulp, then, with a pretense of reluctance, handed over the hard seed. He feared Kani-san’s sharp pincers, should he try to keep both. They parted ways, Saru-san to his lofty pine trees, Kani-san to his quiet home by the stones.

With hopeful patience, Kani-san planted the seed. Spring brought a tender sprout, and with each passing year, it grew, until one autumn, the branches bent heavy with magnificent, golden *kaki*. Kani-san’s heart swelled with joy, watching the fruits ripen, like small suns hanging amidst the leaves.

But alas, Kani-san, with his short legs, could not climb the tall tree. In his dilemma, he remembered Saru-san’s famed agility. He scurried through the bamboo groves and across the stony paths, finding the monkey napping in his favorite *matsu*.

Upon hearing of the bounteous tree, Saru-san's eyes lit up, not with friendship, but with a wicked plan. He ascended the tree, plucking the ripest *kaki* for himself. Then, with a cruel grin, he hurled the hard, unripe fruits down at the waiting Kani-san, striking him again and again until the poor crab lay lifeless at the base of the tree he had so lovingly nurtured.

Saru-san, seeing his wicked deed, fled in a panic, a coward at heart. Soon after, Kani-san’s young son, Waka-kani, discovered his father’s broken body, surrounded by the deadly green *kaki*. His sorrow turned to a burning rage, and he vowed *katakiuchi* – righteous vengeance.

Knowing he could not face the cunning monkey alone, Waka-kani sought help from his father's old friends: Usu-san, the sturdy mortar; Hachi-san, the swift bee; and Kuri-san, the fiery chestnut. They listened to his tale of woe and gladly pledged their aid.

Meanwhile, Saru-san, after days of fearful hiding, convinced himself that no one knew of his crime. "Dead crabs tell no tales!" he chattered, his arrogance returning. But then, a messenger arrived at his dwelling, a small, polite figure. "Saru-san," he bowed, "it is the seventh day since Kani-san’s passing. Waka-kani-sama invites you to a humble memorial feast, for you were his father's dearest friend."

Saru-san feigned deep sorrow, shedding false tears. "Ah, the poor Kani-san! Of course, I shall attend to honor my old companion." He chuckled inwardly, anticipating a free meal.

He arrived at Kani-san’s simple home, where Waka-kani and his family received him with solemn bows. After a feast, Saru-san was led to the *ocha-no-ma* for a calming tea ceremony. As he waited, growing impatient, he approached the *irori*, the sunken hearth, to pour hot water. Suddenly, Kuri-san, hidden in the ashes, burst forth with a "Pop!" striking Saru-san’s neck with a searing heat.

Screaming, Saru-san stumbled back, only to be stung on the cheek by Hachi-san, who had darted from behind the *shōji* screen. Maddened with pain, he bolted for the door. But as he fled through the gate, Usu-san, with the help of some sturdy stones, toppled from above. With a mighty crash, the mortar landed squarely on Saru-san’s head, crushing him to the ground.

Waka-kani then emerged, his sharp pincers gleaming. "Do you remember my father now, Saru-san?" he demanded.

"It... it was... his fault!" gasped the unrepentant monkey, broken and defeated.

"Still you lie!" Waka-kani cried, and with a swift, decisive snip of his powerful claws, he ended Saru-san’s wicked life. Thus, justice was served, and the balance restored.

Kore nite, Saru to Kani no Monogatari wa owari de gozaimasu.
