The Story of the Tiger, the Ram, and the Jackal, as told by Grandmother Grey Wolf

In the time of our ancestors, when the Great Spirit still walked among us, there lived a fierce and solitary hunter, known as Takoda, the Tiger. Takoda roamed the forest, his eyes ever vigilant, his heart ever prepared to strike. One day, while stalking his prey, Takoda chanced upon a clearing, and in the center of it, he saw a majestic being, with horns like the mighty buffalo and a coat of shimmering gold. This was Hanwi, the Ram.

Takoda, with a mixture of fear and respect, approached Hanwi, and asked, "Who are you, noble one?" Hanwi, with a voice like thunder, replied, "I am Hanwi, and you are...?" Takoda, his heart racing, answered, "I am Takoda, the Tiger." And with that, he fled, his paws pounding the earth, his breath coming in ragged gasps.

Takoda rushed to the den of his friend, the cunning and quick-witted Ictinike, the Jackal. "Friend Ictinike," Takoda exclaimed, "I have seen a terrible being, with horns like the mighty elk and a coat of shining gold. His name is Hanwi, and he has frightened me half to death." Ictinike, with a chuckle, replied, "Ah, Takoda, you are as foolish as a young rabbit. Why did you not attack him? We will go tomorrow and feast on his flesh together."

The next day, Takoda and Ictinike set out for Hanwi's clearing, their bellies growling with hunger. As they crested a hill, Hanwi, who had been out grazing, saw them approaching. He quickly called to his wife, the wise and gentle Wokiya, and said, "I fear we are in danger, my love. Takoda and Ictinike are coming for us. What shall we do?" Wokiya, with a calm and gentle voice, replied, "Do not be afraid, my love. Take our child, Little One, in your arms, and pinch him to make him cry. This will distract the hunters and give us time to escape."

Hanwi did as his wife instructed, and as Takoda and Ictinike approached, he called out, "Ah, Takoda, Ictinike! You have brought us food! See how my child cries for sustenance!" Takoda, his heart filled with fear, tried to turn back, but Ictinike, with a cunning smile, bound him to himself with a strip of soft leather. "Come on, Takoda," Ictinike said, "we will feast on Hanwi's flesh together." But Takoda, his senses overwhelmed, fled in terror, dragging Ictinike behind him, through the forest, over hills and valleys, and across rivers. They ran until they were both exhausted and half-dead, and finally, Takoda brought them back to their own den.

And so, Hanwi and his family escaped, their lives spared by the cunning and bravery of Wokiya. From that day on, Takoda and Ictinike told the story of their encounter with Hanwi, a reminder to always respect and honor the power of the great and noble ones.
