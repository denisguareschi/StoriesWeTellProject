My brothers and sisters, gather 'round and listen closely, for I have a tale to tell that will make your hearts pound with excitement. It is the story of the mighty Tiger, the brave Ram, and the cunning Jackal.

In the heart of our beautiful land, where the sun shines bright and the grasslands stretch far and wide, lived these three creatures. Tiger, with his tawny coat and piercing eyes, had gone out to hunt, returning with a rumbling belly and a weary spirit. As he approached the kraal of Ram, he was met with a stern gaze and a gruff voice.

"Who are you, stranger?" Ram asked, his voice like thunder on a stormy night.

"I am Tiger," replied our hero, his voice barely above a whisper.

Ram, with his thick head and mighty horns, struck his breast with his hoof and said, "I am Ram, the guardian of this kraal. You may leave now, Tiger."

But Tiger, fearing Ram's fierce reputation, turned tail and ran, his heart racing like a gazelle's. He rushed back to his home, where his friend Jackal waited with a knowing glint in his eye.

"Ah, Tiger, what's all the commotion about?" Jackal asked, his voice like a chirping bird.

Tiger explained how he had met Ram, and how he had fled in fear. Jackal, with a sly smile, said, "Don't worry, my friend. We'll go back tomorrow and have a feast on Ram's flesh. Ha! He's just a big, lazy bull, anyway."

The next day, the two set off towards Ram's kraal, their bellies rumbling with anticipation. As they crested a hill, Ram, who had been out grazing, saw them approaching and knew that his time was running out.

His wife, a wise and kind creature, whispered in his ear, "Don't be afraid, my love. Take our child in your arms and make it cry, as if it were hungry. That will confuse the strangers and give us time to escape."

Ram did as he was told, and soon Jackal and Tiger appeared on the scene. But when Tiger saw Ram, his fear returned, and he wanted to turn back. Jackal, however, had prepared for this, and had tied Tiger to himself with a strong leathern thong.

"Come on, Tiger," Jackal said, pulling him forward. "We've got to eat, my friend."

Ram, with a loud voice and a clever plan, cried out, "Ah, Jackal, you've brought us Tiger to eat, have you? And look, my child is crying for food! Well done, Jackal, well done!"

Tiger, despite Jackal's entreaties to let him go, was consumed by fear, and he dragged Jackal behind him, racing across the grasslands, leaping over rocks, and crashing through bushes. Jackal, half-dead and battered, begged Tiger to let him go, but our hero was too far gone to listen.

Finally, after many miles of running, Tiger and Jackal returned to their home, exhausted and battered. And Ram, safe and sound, returned to his kraal, his family and his peace of mind intact.

My friends, this is the story of the Tiger, the Ram, and the Jackal. It teaches us that even the bravest of creatures can be fooled by cunning and deceit, and that sometimes, it's better to run with the wind in your hair than to face the dangers that lie ahead.
