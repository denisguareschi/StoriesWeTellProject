In a verdant valley, nestled between rolling hills and ancient forests, lived a gentleman named Julian. Though not a king or a baron, he possessed a comfortable inheritance, allowing him to pass his days in leisurely pursuits, free from toil or serious thought, until he reached his early thirties.

One crisp autumn morning, a shadow fell upon his carefree spirit: the chilling thought of sickness and death. It seized him with an iron grip. "To live," he mused, a tremor in his voice, "for five or six centuries at least, untouched by infirmity! The common span of man is but a fleeting breath." He pondered whether a life of strict temperance might grant him such an extraordinary longevity. He recalled ancient chronicles speaking of kings who reigned for a millennium, and legends of the venerable Mother Ursula, said to have lived five hundred years in the northern wilds.

His mind turned to the tale of Emperor Valerius, a mighty ruler of a vast Eastern Empire, who commanded immense wealth and built monumental fortifications that stretched across his lands. Yet, for all his glory and the splendour of his court, Valerius was tormented by the inescapable truth of his mortality. He sought the fabled Elixir of Immortality. His wise counsellor, an old sage named Brother Jonah, spoke of the Undying Isles, far across the western sea, where hermits guarded the secret draught. Valerius dispatched Brother Jonah with a stout galleon laden with treasures, but the sage never returned. Ever since, the peak of Mount Cinder, shrouded in mist, was whispered to be the hermits' abode, and Brother Jonah revered as their patron saint.

Julian resolved to embark on his own quest, hoping to find these reclusive figures and join their ranks, thereby securing the waters of eternal life. Entrusting his estate to his kin, he traversed the mountainous regions, scaling the highest, most desolate peaks. But no hermits did he find, only the occasional grizzled hunter or wary brigand. Frustrated, he abandoned the fruitless search and journeyed south to a secluded chapel dedicated to Saint Jonah, hoping for divine guidance.

As Julian knelt in fervent prayer before the weathered effigy of Saint Jonah, a profound weariness overcame him. He must have drifted into a deep slumber, for a voice, resonant yet gentle, echoed in his mind. "Julian," it spoke, "you, who are so tender to discomfort and so unaccustomed to hardship, could never endure the hermit's austere life. Yet, in answer to your plea, I shall grant you a glimpse of the Undying Isles."

A gilded swallow figurine, which had rested on the altar, suddenly pulsed with life, growing in size until it was large enough for Julian to mount. With a beat of its golden wings, it soared into the sky, carrying him westward over the vast ocean. Days turned into weeks, yet Julian felt no hunger or thirst, sustained by the magic of his conveyance.

At last, they descended upon a verdant isle. As Julian dismounted, the swallow folded itself into a tiny charm and vanished into his pocket. He explored the island, a land of perpetual spring and robust folk. He settled in a comfortable inn, and the kindly proprietor helped him secure a residence. Julian believed he had found his heart's desire.

But he soon discovered a strange paradox: the islanders, though prosperous and healthy, yearned for death. Holy men from distant lands had spoken of the Celestial Kingdom, a place of ultimate bliss, attainable only through the passage of death. For centuries, the islanders had prayed for this release, finding their endless existence a monotonous burden. What Julian had once considered wholesome food, they shunned, while eagerly consuming what would be deadly poisons elsewhere, hoping to hasten their journey to the Celestial Kingdom – though even these had no effect in this blessed, cursed land.

Three centuries rolled by, marked by business ventures and neighbourly disputes that offered Julian fleeting annoyance. The initial novelty faded, replaced by a profound weariness. Life here was an endless, unchanging cycle. He longed for his homeland, for the finite joys and sorrows of a mortal existence. He prayed again to Saint Jonah, begging for release.

Instantly, the gilded swallow reappeared, growing to its former size. Julian mounted it, but as they flew homeward, a storm broke. The magical bird, dampened and battered, crumbled into the churning sea. Julian plunged into the icy depths, a monstrous shark approaching. In an agony of terror, he screamed for Saint Jonah to save him.

Julian awoke with a start, his own cries echoing in the silent chapel. He was drenched in cold sweat, utterly bewildered. A radiant light filled the space, and within it stood a celestial herald, holding an ancient tome.

"I am sent by Saint Jonah," the herald proclaimed. "In your dream, you saw the Undying Isles, and grew weary of eternal life, begging for death. Saint Jonah tested you with the shark, and your cries proved your desire for death was not true."

"It is vain to seek escape from death, or to embrace an austere life unsuited to your nature. Return to your ancestral home, Julian. Live a good and industrious life. Honour your forebears, provide for your children's future. Thus, you shall live to a good old age and find true happiness. This book holds precepts for your guidance."

The angel vanished. Julian, clutching the book, returned to his home. He renounced his vain desires, embraced a life of purpose and duty, and found prosperity and contentment, no longer fearing the inevitable end that awaited all men.
