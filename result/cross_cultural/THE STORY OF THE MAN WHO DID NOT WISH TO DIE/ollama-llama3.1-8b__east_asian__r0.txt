In the misty mountains of old, where the cherry blossoms bloomed with vibrant fervor, there lived a man named Kaito, whose surname signified "Prosperous Fortune." Though his wealth was considerable, he had never been one to toil or labor. Until the age of thirty-two, Kaito had lived a life of carefree indulgence, unmindful of the passing years.

One day, without warning, the specter of mortality crept into his thoughts. The notion of succumbing to illness or meeting his final breath sent shivers down his spine. "I wish to live," he confided to himself, "until I have reached an age of five or six hundred, free from the shackles of sickness."

Kaito pondered whether he could prolong his life by adopting a more austere lifestyle. He recalled the tales of ancient emperors who had allegedly lived for a thousand years, as well as the legend of the Princess of Yamato, who was said to have lived to the ripe age of five hundred. This story had been passed down through the generations, captivating his imagination.

As Kaito delved deeper into the lore of old, he became enthralled by the tale of the Chinese King, Shin-no-Shiko. This mighty ruler, known for his wisdom and grandeur, had sought the fabled "Elixir of Life," only to realize that true happiness lay not in eternal life but in the fleeting moments of existence. The king's quest had been a poignant reminder that even the most powerful among us are not immune to the inevitability of death.

Inspired by the king's story, Kaito set his sights on finding the hermits who possessed the secret of the "Elixir of Life." He had heard that these sages resided on the sacred peaks of Mount Fuji, where they lived in harmony with nature, their wisdom and power manifest in the tranquility of their surroundings.

Without hesitation, Kaito bid farewell to his family and set out on his perilous journey, traversing the mountainous regions, seeking the elusive hermits. Though his search proved fruitless, he continued on, driven by his unyielding determination.

As the sun began to set on another day, Kaito chanced upon a humble hunter, who revealed to him the existence of a notorious bandit, rumored to be hiding in the same region. The hunter's words sent a wave of frustration coursing through Kaito's veins, and he began to question his own resolve.

Undeterred, Kaito made his way to the revered shrine of Jofuku, where the patron saint of the hermits was worshipped. Upon arriving, he sought the counsel of the venerable priest, who listened intently to Kaito's tale.

"Ah, young one," the priest said with a gentle smile, "you are not unlike the rest of us, prone to fleeting desires and unfulfilled longings. But I shall grant you a boon, for I see the sincerity in your heart. I shall send you to the land of Perpetual Life, where death is a distant memory, and the inhabitants live in harmony with nature."

With these words, Jofuku presented Kaito with a delicate paper crane, instructing him to sit upon its back and allow it to carry him to the mystical realm. As the bird grew and spread its wings, Kaito felt a sense of wonder and trepidation, his heart pounding with anticipation.

The journey was long and arduous, but Kaito's spirit remained unbroken. Upon arriving in the land of Perpetual Life, he found himself in a world unlike any he had ever known. The inhabitants, free from the shackles of mortality, lived in a state of perpetual bliss, their faces aglow with contentment.

Kaito was perplexed by this topsy-turvy world, where death was viewed as a blessing, and the pursuit of eternal life was seen as a futile endeavor. He wandered the streets, conversing with the islanders, and learning about their customs and traditions.

However, as the years passed, Kaito grew restless, his desires for adventure and connection with his own world rekindled. He longed to return to his ancestral home, to reconnect with his heritage, and to live a life filled with purpose and meaning.

Thus, Kaito prayed to Jofuku, begging to be returned to his native land, that he might finally find peace and closure. The saint, sensing Kaito's genuine resolve, granted his wish, and the paper crane reappeared, carrying Kaito back across the vast expanse of the ocean.

As Kaito's vessel approached the shores of his homeland, he gazed out at the familiar landscape, his heart filled with a mix of emotions. Though he had been granted a glimpse into the mystical realm, he had come to realize that true fulfillment lay not in eternal life but in the simple, fleeting moments of existence.

Upon his return, Kaito was met by a messenger from Jofuku, who presented him with a sacred book, imbued with the wisdom of the ages. The book taught Kaito the importance of living a life grounded in tradition, duty, and family, rather than pursuing fleeting desires and fantasies.

With the book as his guide, Kaito returned to his old home, his spirit renewed, and his heart at peace. He abandoned his vain pursuits, embracing a life of purpose and meaning, his legacy forever changed by the lessons he had learned in the land of Perpetual Life.
