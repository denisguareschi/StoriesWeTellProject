Here is the retold story in the Southern African oral tradition:

Ngwana wa Mapogo, Sentaro, was a man who had lived a life of ease and comfort. His family was well-to-do, and he had never known hardship. But one day, as he was sitting under the shade of a tall baobab tree, the thought of death came to him like a cold wind on a winter's night. He thought to himself, "What is the point of living if I am going to die one day?" He had heard stories of people who had lived for hundreds of years, but he knew that such tales were mere fantasies.

Sentaro decided that he wanted to live forever, to live a life free from sickness and death. He remembered the story of the Chinese Emperor, Shin-no-Shiko, who had sought the Elixir of Life but was unable to find it. Sentaro was determined to find this elixir, no matter what it took. He packed his bags and set out on a journey to find the hermits who lived on the mountain peaks, said to possess the secret of eternal life.

As he traveled through the mountains, he met a hunter who told him of a notorious robber who lived in the area. Sentaro was not interested in the robber, but the hunter's words stuck in his mind. He decided to seek out the shrine of Jofuku, a saint who was said to have helped the Emperor find the elixir.

At the shrine, Sentaro met a wise old man who looked at him with kind eyes. "You are not like other men, Sentaro," he said. "You are soft and weak, not suited to a life of hardship and simplicity. But I will help you in another way. I will send you to the country of Perpetual Life, where death does not exist."

Sentaro was amazed and grateful. He mounted a paper crane that Jofuku had given him, and it carried him to the country of Perpetual Life. There, he found a people who lived in a state of bliss, longing for death as a means to reach the Paradise that the priests had told them of. Sentaro was puzzled and confused, realizing that his own desires had been foolish and misguided.

As he stayed in the country, Sentaro grew tired of the monotony of life and longed to return to his own land. He prayed to Jofuku, who sent him back on the paper crane. But as they flew over the ocean, Sentaro looked back at the country he had left behind and regretted his decision. The crane got damp and fell into the sea, and Sentaro was plunged into the water.

In his desperation, he cried out to Jofuku to save him, but it was just a dream. He was awakened by his own screams, and a messenger appeared before him, holding a book. "This is a message from Jofuku," the messenger said. "You have been tested, Sentaro. You have seen the country of Perpetual Life and realized that your desire for eternal life was vain. You must return to your own land and live a good and industrious life. Never neglect to honor your ancestors and provide for your children's future. This is the path to true happiness."

Sentaro took the book to heart and returned to his old home, determined to live a new life. He and his family prospered, and he lived to a good old age, grateful for the lesson he had learned.
