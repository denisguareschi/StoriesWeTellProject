In the time before time, when the sun was young and the forests were dark, the great spirit, Wisaakodewin, created the world and all its creatures. Among them, two brothers, Takoda, the lion, and Wanbli, the jackal, roamed the land.

Takoda had caught a mighty buffalo, which lay still on the high riverbank. Takoda, his throat parched and his belly growling, went to the river to drink. "Wanbli, my nephew," he called out, "watch over the buffalo while I quench my thirst. Do not eat of it, lest it be a sin."

Wanbli, with his cunning and sly ways, agreed to keep watch. But while Takoda was away, Wanbli and his wife, Kaga, ate their fill of the buffalo's meat. When Takoda returned, he found the path blocked by a large stone, which Wanbli had cleverly rolled onto the trail. Takoda roared in frustration, "Wanbli, help me! I need to climb up!"

Wanbli, feigning kindness, said, "Uncle Takoda, I will help you. I will let down a rope, and you can climb up." But he whispered to Kaga, "Give me a rope as thin as a willow branch," while shouting, "Give me a rope as strong as the buffalo's hide, my wife!" Kaga, unaware of Wanbli's true intentions, gave him a rope as weak as a spider's web.

Takoda climbed up, but the rope snapped, and he fell to the bottom, his great body crashing onto the rocks. Wanbli, feigning concern, shouted, "Uncle Takoda, I will help you again!" But he whispered to Kaga, "Give me another rope, as rotten as the wood of the old cedar tree," while shouting, "Give me a rope as strong as the mighty cedar, my wife!" Kaga, still unaware, gave him another weak rope.

Takoda climbed up once more, but the rope broke again, and he fell once more. Wanbli, still pretending to help, beat on a dry hide, crying out, "My wife, why did you give me such a bad rope? Didn't I tell you to give me a strong one?" Takoda roared, "Wanbli, stop beating your wife! I will hurt you if you don't cease. Help me to climb up!"

Wanbli, still playing the fool, said, "Uncle Takoda, I will help you. I will give you a rope." But he whispered to Kaga, "Give me the worst rope you have," while shouting, "Give me the strongest rope, my wife!" Kaga, finally understanding, gave him a rope as weak as a gnat's thread.

Takoda climbed up once more, but Wanbli gave a mighty tug, and the rope broke. Takoda fell to the bottom, his great body broken and still. Wanbli and Kaga slowly walked away, leaving Takoda to his fate. As they disappeared into the darkness, Takoda's spirit rose up, and he spoke to the Great Spirit, Wisaakodewin, "Why did you create such a cruel and heartless nephew?" Wisaakodewin replied, "Takoda, you should have trusted the spirits of the land. Wanbli is a trickster, but he is also a reminder that the world is full of dangers and that one must always be mindful and clever to survive."
