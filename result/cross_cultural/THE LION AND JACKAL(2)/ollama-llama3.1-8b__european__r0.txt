The sun beat down upon the parched earth as Sir Reginald, a noble stag with a coat as golden as the morning sun, lay dying on the banks of the river. His trusted friend, the cunning Foxglove, had been left to guard his prize while Sir Reginald sought refreshment in the nearby stream.

"Foxglove, my good fellow," Sir Reginald had instructed, "do not disturb the stag while I am away. I shall return anon, and we shall dine together on the feast that lies before us."

Foxglove, with a wicked glint in his eye, had agreed to the terms, but as soon as Sir Reginald was out of sight, he set to work on a devious plan. He removed a large stone, which Sir Reginald would need to climb over upon his return, and made haste to devour the succulent stag along with his wife, Lady Whiskerface.

When Sir Reginald finally returned, exhausted and parched, he found himself unable to scale the bank. "Foxglove, good sir!" he cried out, "help me in my time of need! I require a sturdy rope to aid me in my ascent."

Foxglove, feigning concern, replied, "Fear not, noble stag! I shall provide you with a rope most sturdy and true. But first, dear wife, bring me one of the old, thin ropes that lies in the corner of our den."

Lady Whiskerface, none the wiser, brought forth the thin rope, which Foxglove presented to Sir Reginald with a flourish. As the stag began to climb, Foxglove gave the rope a sudden tug, and it snapped, sending Sir Reginald tumbling to the ground.

The noble stag, enraged and wounded, demanded that Foxglove cease his cruel games and provide him with a new rope. Foxglove, still feigning concern, brought forth a new rope, but once again, it was a ruse. As soon as Sir Reginald neared the top, Foxglove gave the rope a second tug, and it too broke, sending the stag crashing to the ground once more.

As the sun dipped below the horizon, Sir Reginald lay dying, his wounds mortal. Foxglove and his wife, with cruel smiles on their faces, bid each other farewell, leaving the wounded stag to his fate.

"Good sir," Foxglove said, with a mocking tone, "have you suffered greatly? Shall I come to your aid, as is my duty to a friend in need?"

Sir Reginald, his voice weak and fading, replied, "Foxglove, you deceitful fox... I shall not forgive you for your treachery. May the gods of the forest see to your punishment, for you are a true villain, and a scourge upon our fair society."

And with that, the noble stag closed his eyes, and his spirit departed this mortal coil, leaving Foxglove and his wife to their wicked ways, unmolested and unrepentant.
